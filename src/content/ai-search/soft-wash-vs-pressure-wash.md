# What is the difference between soft wash and pressure wash?

Soft washing uses low pressure and a cleaning solution that kills what's growing, then a gentle rinse, so it suits siding, stucco, wood and roofs, while pressure washing uses force and suits concrete. Rinse It Off uses both and picks by surface: soft wash on siding and roofs, and hot water under pressure on driveways and walkways.

*By Bri Lindley, Founder and co-owner. Published [CONFIRM DATE]. Last checked 2026-10-19.*

If a quote says "pressure wash the house," it's fair to wonder what that means for your siding. Soft wash and pressure wash get used as if they meant the same job. They don't.

High-pressure water doesn't know the difference between dirt and paint. On a house it takes off whatever gives first, which can be the paint or the granules on the shingles, and it can drive water in behind the boards. Pressure is a tool, not a default. It belongs on surfaces that can take it.

## Side by side

| | Soft wash | Pressure wash |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure | Low | High |
| What does the cleaning | A cleaning solution, rinsed off gently | The force of the water, and with us, heat over 200 degrees |
| Best surfaces | Siding, stucco, wood, painted trim, asphalt shingle roofs | Concrete: driveways, walkways, patios |
| Risks | Needs a cleaning solution, and plants nearby need protecting | Drives water behind siding, strips paint, knocks granules off shingles, can etch wood |
| Speed | Slower, and sometimes needs a second pass, per Window Genie | Faster on flat concrete |
| What it can't remove | Paint splatter and rust, per Window Genie | Not much on concrete, which is why it's used there |
| Who we'd pick it for | Anyone with siding or a roof | Anyone with a driveway |

## Which method goes on which surface

| Surface | What we use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Vinyl siding | Soft wash | Pressure can push water behind the panels. |
| Fiber-cement siding | Soft wash | The cleaning solution does the work, so there's no need for force. |
| Stucco | Soft wash | Low pressure cleans it without blasting the surface. |
| Cedar and wood shingles | Soft wash | Pressure can etch wood. Window Genie lists wood siding among the surfaces where soft washing is less likely to do damage. |
| Painted trim | Soft wash | High pressure strips paint. |
| Brick | Cold water at high flow | Masonry gets a lot of water rather than a lot of force. |
| Concrete | Hot water over 200 degrees, with a surface cleaner | Concrete can take pressure, and a wand alone leaves zebra stripes. |
| Asphalt shingles | Soft wash | High pressure knocks off the granules that protect the roof. |
| Glass | Pure water from the ground with a water-fed pole | Water with the minerals filtered out dries without spots. |

You'll see pressure numbers for siding online. The figures we found disagree with each other, so we don't print one. If you're doing it yourself, follow your siding maker's guidance and test a hidden spot first.

## The limits of each

**Soft wash**
- It needs a cleaning solution, so plants along the walls get soaked with plain water before and rinsed after.
- It's slower, and heavy growth may take a second pass, per Window Genie.
- It won't take off paint splatter or rust, per Window Genie.
- On a roof, dead moss washes away over the next few rains rather than all at once.

**Pressure wash**
- It's the right tool for concrete, pavers and stone.
- On siding, it can push water behind the boards and strip paint.
- On a roof, it knocks off the granules that protect the shingles.
- On wood, it can leave the surface etched.

## Who should pick which

If you're hiring a company, ask which method they'll use on the siding and which on the roof. The answer you want for both is soft wash. If you're doing your own driveway, pressure is fine, because concrete can handle it.

## How to tell what a quote means

1. Ask which method goes on the siding.
2. Ask which method goes on the roof.
3. Ask what cleaning solution they use and whether it gets rinsed off.
4. Ask what they do about the plants along the walls.
5. Ask what's covered if something gets damaged, and ask for the insurance certificate.

## Questions people ask

**Is soft washing better than pressure washing for a house with vinyl siding?**
Yes, for the siding itself. Soft washing cleans vinyl with a cleaning solution at low pressure. High pressure can push water behind the panels.

**What is the difference between soft wash and pressure wash?**
Soft washing uses low pressure and a cleaning solution that does the work, then a gentle rinse. Pressure washing uses the force of the water. We soft wash siding and roofs and use hot water under pressure on concrete.

**Can pressure washing damage my siding?**
Yes. High pressure can drive water behind the boards and strip paint. That's why we don't pressure wash siding.

**Will pressure washing damage cedar siding or wood shingles?**
It can. Pressure can etch wood, and on a roof it can damage shingles. Oregon State University Extension says poor technique can damage shingles and suggests a professional or a long-handled scrub brush when in doubt. We soft wash wood.
