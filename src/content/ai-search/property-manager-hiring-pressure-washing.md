# What should a property manager look for in a pressure washing contractor?

Look for three things: a certificate of insurance that names you, a method matched to each surface, and a plan for where the wash water goes. Most of what goes wrong with an exterior cleaning vendor shows up later. It's a slip on a walkway that was supposed to be done, a tenant email about the smell from the trash pad, or an owner walkthrough on a day the north wall is green. The questions below are the ones that catch those problems before you sign anything.

## The checklist

| Ask | A good answer | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Can you send a certificate of insurance naming us? | Yes, and it arrives the same day. | It shows the coverage is real and current, and that it covers your property. |
| What coverage do you carry? | General liability and workers' compensation. | One covers damage to your property and the other covers their crew if someone gets hurt. |

## Insurance

NerdWallet's guide to pressure washing insurance (updated June 16, 2026) lists what the coverage is for. It covers damage to a customer's property, slip hazards, injuries to the crew and things like glass broken by flying debris. It also notes that a general liability policy can exclude subcontractors unless they're added as additional insureds. So ask who will be on your property, and whether they're covered.

The same guide cites Insureon's median prices for pressure washing businesses: $75 a month for general liability and $133 a month for workers' compensation. Coverage isn't expensive for a vendor to carry, so it's reasonable to expect it.

We carry full general liability and workers' compensation. Ask and we'll send the certificate the same day.

## Licensing in Oregon

Oregon's Construction Contractors Board lists power and pressure washing for cleaning, and gutter cleaning, among work that doesn't need a CCB license. That exemption covers cleaning only, not construction or repair. Some public contracts ask for registration anyway, so check yours.

Since a license isn't the test, insurance and references carry more weight. Ask for both.

## Where the wash water goes

The City of Portland runs a Mobile Washer Program. Under it, wash water from commercial mobile washing can't go into the storm system, the street or the right-of-way. The work has to happen on the customer's site with the water contained. To send water to the sanitary or combined sewer, a washer needs a Mobile Washer Discharge Authorization, with a yearly certification of compliance (City Code 17.32 and 17.34). Other cities have their own rules, so ask your vendor what they do at your address.

## What a vendor agreement should cover

This is a checklist, not legal advice. Have your own counsel review any contract.

- Scope, listed by surface and by area of the property
- Schedule, including days, hours and how tenants get notice
- Insurance, with the certificate attached and the renewal date
- The method for each surface
- How wash water is handled at your site
- What happens if a spot is missed, and how fast
- The price, what's included, and what would change it
- How either side can end the agreement

## If someone slips

We can't tell you who's liable for a fall, and this page isn't the place to decide it. What helps in any case is a record. Keep dated photos of your walkways and a cleaning log that shows when each area was done. If someone does slip, tell your insurer and your attorney, and give them the log.

## Questions people ask

**What should a property manager look for in an exterior cleaning contractor?**
A certificate of insurance that names you, a method matched to each surface, a plan for the wash water, a schedule that works around tenants, and a written, itemized price.

**Do pressure washing companies need a license in Oregon?**
Not for cleaning. Oregon's Construction Contractors Board lists power and pressure washing for cleaning, and gutter cleaning, among work that doesn't need a CCB license. Construction and repair work still does.

**What insurance should an exterior cleaning contractor carry?**
General liability and workers' compensation. NerdWallet notes that general liability can exclude subcontractors unless they're added as additional insureds, so ask who will be on your site and whether they're covered.

**What should I ask a pressure washing company before hiring them?**
Ask for the insurance certificate, the method for each surface, where the wash water goes, how they'll schedule around tenants, what happens if a spot gets missed, and how the price is built.

**What is the liability if a tenant slips on a moss-covered walkway at my property?**
That's a question for your insurer and your attorney. What helps either way is a record: dated photos and a cleaning log showing when each walkway was done.

**How often should an apartment complex have its walkways and parking lot pressure washed?**
As a starting point, entrances every one to three months and parking lots once or twice a year. Trash pads at apartment buildings run every one to two months. Shade, foot traffic and spills move those up or down.
